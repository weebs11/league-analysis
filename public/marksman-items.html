THE ONE RULE

Buy for the fight you need to win—not for the prettiest six-item screenshot.

Damage is only useful if you can deliver it. Start by asking whether the next fight needs burst, tank damage, survival, or access. Then buy the item that removes that constraint.

CAITLYN, SPECIFICALLY

Build around the shot
that actually lands.

Caitlyn converts range, critical strikes, and heavy AD into threatening Headshots. She wants clean single-target hits—not a pile of on-hit effects she struggles to apply repeatedly.

Bow if safe.
Blade if contested.

Doran’s Bow is the damage-forward bet. Take Blade when 80 health will decide whether you survive hard trades or an engage lane.

FIRST ITEM

Choose your lane out.

Hexoptics rewards range and skirmish resets. Yun Tal is the scaling auto-attack path. Stormrazor buys one safer hit and a burst of repositioning.

03

CONVERT

IE, then answer armor.

Infinity Edge turns Caitlyn’s crit and Headshot pattern into real threat. Add LDR early when frontliners are already stacking armor; add RFC when access is the problem.

STAY ALIVE

Defend against the cause.

Shieldbow for burst, Bloodthirster for attrition, Mercurial for one removable disable, Guardian Angel for an all-in that your team can punish.

“I can hit from max range.”

Hexoptics C44

Its attack damage scales with distance and its takedown window extends your range. Caitlyn naturally plays where both clauses work.

“Their front line is taking over.”

LDR before luxury

Percentage armor penetration and Giant Slayer usually beat another comfort crit item once bonus health and armor arrive.

“I cannot walk into range.”

Rapid Firecannon

One extended-range attack is often enough to collect a trapped Headshot, tag a carry, or begin a fight without volunteering your life.

“The guide says it’s core.”

Check the actual game

A live assassin, a fed tank, or unavoidable crowd control can make defense or penetration worth far more than the highest average damage purchase.

SCOPE & METHOD

Current facts, clearly separated from judgment.

Item facts—names, costs, stats, and effects—come from Riot’s live NA Data Dragon payload for version 16.16.1, aligned to gameplay patch 26.16. Caitlyn fit is an editorial assessment of kit synergy and game-state purpose, not a claim about current win rate.

Riot’s public item API does not expose shop class, so classification was checked against the patch-pinned client item records: ItemData.mItemAttributes value 2 is the Marksman flag. The completed-item filter yields exactly 26 live items. CommunityDragon is used only as a mirror/extractor for those Riot-shipped client assets; visible stats and effects are cross-checked against Riot Data Dragon.

Basic components, consumables, Ornn/Role Quest upgrades, Arena/ARAM items, deprecated IDs, and removed items are excluded from the main count. Starter choices and practical boot pivots are shown separately so the report remains useful from first buy to full build.
